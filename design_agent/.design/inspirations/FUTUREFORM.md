# INSPIRATION — FUTUREFORM
> futureform.uk — Salesforce consultancy for UK financial services
> Extract: B2B services layout, industry-segmented navigation, trust badge clusters, financial sector credibility patterns

---

## WHAT FUTUREFORM DOES DIFFERENTLY

Futureform solves a hard B2B communication problem: a single technology platform (Salesforce) serving four distinct industries, each with different needs. The design must make each buyer feel specifically addressed without fragmenting the brand. Study this for industry-segmented service architecture.

---

## SPACING PHILOSOPHY

**Observation:** Generous whitespace signals systematic thinking — appropriate for financial clients.

- Section separations are wide — each content block is a distinct chapter
- Industry cards maintain consistent internal padding — uniformity implies reliability
- No visual density for density's sake — sparse layouts signal precision over volume

**Extract:** For financial services B2B, whitespace is a trust signal. Dense layouts feel rushed; spacious layouts feel considered. The spacing itself communicates the brand value of "precision."

---

## TYPOGRAPHY RHYTHM

**Observation:** Sans-serif, professional, readable — optimized for scanning professionals.

- Neutral blues/greens likely — financial sector color conventions are there for trust reasons
- Clean hierarchy: section title → subhead → body — no typographic experimentation
- CTAs ("Learn more," "Contact us") restrained and consistent

**Extract:** In regulated or financial contexts, typographic conservatism IS the right call. Don't introduce personality through fonts when the buyer is a compliance officer or fund manager. Readable = trustworthy.

---

## INTERACTION DENSITY

**Observation:** Dropdown navigation under "Industries" — segmentation happens at nav level.

- Industry-specific entry points in navigation — buyer finds their vertical immediately
- Numbered industry blocks (01–04) — ordered list implies comprehensive coverage
- Testimonials, case studies, event promotions — multiple trust-building interaction types on one page

**Extract:** For multi-vertical B2B services, put industry segmentation in the primary navigation. Don't make buyers hunt for their vertical. The faster they self-identify, the more relevant everything downstream feels.

---

## INFORMATION HIERARCHY

**Observation:** Page order mirrors the buyer's decision journey.

1. Positioning: what Futureform does
2. Industries: which buyers they serve
3. Social proof: testimonials
4. Trust indicators: partner badges + ratings
5. Events: ongoing engagement opportunities

**Extract:** B2B service page order = positioning → segmentation → proof → credentials → next engagement. "Next engagement" (webinar, event) keeps buyers who aren't ready to contact.

---

## EMOTIONAL TONE

**Observation:** "Transparent and collaborative" positioning differentiates from typical vendor relationship.

- Partnership framing: they understand "what financial services companies really need"
- Not "we implement Salesforce" but "we speak your language"
- Industry insider tone — not generic tech consultant

**Extract:** B2B differentiation often lives in copy, not design. "We understand your industry" beats "we have expertise" — specificity creates psychological fit. Design frames it; copy closes it.

---

## TRUST PATTERNS

**Observation:** Trust badge cluster — partner tier + rating visible together.

- "Summit Partner" + "5 Star Rating" badges cluster — each amplifies the other
- Case study cards with real company imagery — named success stories outperform generic testimonials
- UK-specific positioning — local market knowledge signals = local trust signals

**Extract:** Partner tiers and ratings should appear together, not separately — the combination creates compound credibility. Alone, either one is easy to dismiss.

---

## MOTION PHILOSOPHY

**Observation:** Professional services site — motion likely minimal or absent.

- No ambient motion expected — financial services buyers are suspicious of visual flash
- Dropdown interactions: likely simple fade/slide, functional not decorative
- CTAs static — no hover animations that could feel pushy

**Extract:** For financial B2B, absence of motion is a design decision. Still, precise, reliable. Motion would undermine the positioning.

---

## WHAT NOT TO EXTRACT

- Salesforce partner badge — only relevant if you're actually a certified partner
- UK financial services specific copy patterns — localisation is context-specific
- Webinar/event sections — only add if you'll actually maintain them; stale events destroy credibility
- Four-industry block structure — works for Futureform because they serve exactly four verticals; force-fitting other numbers fails
