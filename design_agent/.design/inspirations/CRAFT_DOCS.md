# INSPIRATION — CRAFT
> craft.do — Document editor for Mac and iOS
> Extract: editorial polish, typographic hierarchy, warmth through precision

---

## WHY CRAFT MATTERS

Craft shows how to make a productivity tool feel beautiful without compromising function. Where Notion feels calm and neutral, Craft feels warm and crafted. It's a case study in emotional product design — where visual polish communicates quality and the product becomes a pleasure to use rather than just a tool.

---

## SPACING PHILOSOPHY

**Observation:** Craft uses proportional, generous spacing that mirrors editorial publishing.

- Content width: carefully constrained even on large screens
- Paragraph spacing: generous — documents feel like printed material
- UI chrome: tight and compact, contrasting against the generous editorial space
- The contrast between tight UI and open content creates a clear "stage" for the document

**Extract:** Create contrast between UI density and content density. Tight chrome + open content = the product is in service of the content, not competing with it.

---

## TYPOGRAPHY RHYTHM

**Observation:** Craft treats typography with editorial seriousness.

- Body: warm, large enough to read comfortably, generous line-height
- Heading hierarchy is visually clear and satisfying — each level has distinct presence
- Consistent vertical rhythm — spacing above/below elements follows a grid
- Supports a range of type choices (user-selectable fonts), but the defaults are excellent

**Extract:** Default typography choices communicate product quality before the user changes anything. A product with poor default typography signals poor craftsmanship throughout. Invest in the defaults.

---

## WARMTH THROUGH DETAIL

**Observation:** Craft achieves warmth through micro-details, not color or illustration.

- Subtle paper texture or very slight warm tint on surfaces
- Shadows on cards: warm-tinted (not pure gray)
- Icons: slightly rounded, organic rather than geometric
- Hover states: smooth, immediate, satisfying — never jarring
- The sum of small quality signals creates an overall "crafted" feeling

**Extract:** Warmth in UI comes from the accumulated quality of small decisions — not from slapping warm colors on everything. Each micro-detail (shadow color, icon rounding, hover smoothness) contributes 1%. Sum of 50 details = 50% warmer product.

---

## EDITING EXPERIENCE

**Observation:** Craft's editing surface is deeply considered.

- Selection: custom, elegant — feels better than browser default
- Block handles: unobtrusive, precise
- Drag feedback: smooth, with clear drop-zone indicators
- Toolbar: contextual, appears near selection, not fixed top bar
- The editor itself is the product — the UI is its frame

**Extract:** In editor-primary products, invest disproportionately in the editing surface interaction quality. Users will feel every rough edge. Contextual toolbars near the selection point feel more natural than fixed toolbars for content editors.

---

## DARK MODE AS AESTHETIC

**Observation:** Craft's dark mode is designed with as much care as light mode.

- Not a simple color inversion
- Warm dark surfaces (slightly warm-tinted neutrals, not cool grays)
- Elevation through lightness (cards lighter than page)
- Typography remains warm and readable
- Illustrations and UI elements adapted for dark context (not just recolored)

**Extract:** Dark mode is a separate design discipline. Products that want to be premium must design dark mode as a first-class mode, not an afterthought. Warm dark surfaces (using stone/zinc neutrals rather than slate) maintain brand warmth even at night.

---

## TRUST THROUGH QUALITY

**Observation:** Every detail in Craft communicates that it was made with care.

- No obvious bugs in the core experience
- Consistent padding everywhere
- Animations feel right (not just present)
- Copy is precise and warm
- App icon, website, product: same quality level throughout

**Extract:** Trust from quality is built when every layer of the product maintains the same standard. A beautifully designed screen with a broken loading state destroys the quality signal. Total quality beats selective quality.

---

## WHAT NOT TO EXTRACT

- The Mac-native design language — Craft is heavily platform-specific (works for them, may not fit web)
- The block types and document structure — product-specific
- The specific warmth level — Craft leans very warm, which is product-specific. Extract the principle (warmth through detail) not the specific temperature.
- The iOS-style design patterns — don't import mobile patterns into web products without adaptation
